# The figure kit

The people of the starter asset library are built by these scripts: voxel figures assembled from
pieces on one skeleton. Nothing is generated by a service and nothing is modelled by hand, so a new
race, outfit or hair style is a change to a script and a rebuild.

This page is the kit's style guide and build manual, for people and for agents (the editor's AI
agent server serves it as `rustpg2d://docs/figure-kit`).

## The look

- **Voxels, one per pixel.** The game draws a person 80 image pixels tall (40 world pixels, two
  image pixels each). A kit voxel is 0.0225 model units at a height of 1.7, which is exactly one
  of those pixels. Finer detail would not show; coarser detail looks blocky beside the rest.
- **Realistic proportions.** About seven and a half heads tall. No big heads.
- **Flat colours.** Each piece uses a handful of plain colours from `figure.py`. No textures, no
  gradients, no dithering: the engine's cel shading adds the light and dark.
- **A silhouette that tells who it is.** Ears, tusks, horns, a tail, a beard, a helmet's crest:
  what sets a figure apart must change its outline, because a face is ten pixels wide.
- **Edges one voxel soft.** Limbs, torso and head lose their corner voxels, so a figure reads as
  round at game size.

## How a figure is put together

| What | Where it comes from | Engine |
|---|---|---|
| The skeleton and the 14 clips | `rig/human.glb` (the hero's rig; `rig/extract.py` wrote it) | `models/kit_clips.glb`, shared by every body (`shared_clips`: fitted to each body's bone lengths) |
| A **build**: proportions | `BUILDS` in `kit.py` (medium, dwarf, small, large; each for men and women) | bone lengths of the body and of its outfits |
| A **body**: head, neck and what the race adds (ears, tusks, a dragon's head, horns, a tail) | `BODIES` in `kit.py`, `Figure.body` | a 3D model that is a figure, with its own pieces (`parts`) |
| A **piece**: an outfit, hair, a beard, headwear, a cloak | `Figure.outfit`, `hair`, `beard`, `headwear`, `cloak` | a 3D model with `part` (its slot, its style, the bodies it fits) |

A piece is bound to the same bones as the body, so it moves with every clip. The engine draws the
body and its pieces in one pose and recolours them together.

- **Outfits fit one build** (they follow its bone lengths), so there is one per build and sex.
- **Head pieces fit a head size.** Heads are 10 voxels, the small build's 8, so hair, beards and
  headwear come in two sizes and fit every body with that head.
- **Slots:** `outfit`, `hair`, `beard`, `headwear`, `cloak`. A figure wears one piece per slot.
- **Styles** name a piece across bodies: `clothes`, `leather_armor`, `chain_mail`, `plate_armor`,
  `robe`, `hair_short`, `beard_long`, `helmet`, `hood`, `cloak`. An item's *Shown as* names a
  style; each body then wears its own piece of it.

## Colours

Colour features recolour exact colours, so pieces share the constants in `figure.py`:

- `SKIN` (with `MOUTH` and `BELLY` as its darker and lighter shades) is the feature **skin**.
  Every body and outfit is built in the same base skin colour; a race's own skin is the *default*
  of the body's skin feature, and the engine gives it to the body's pieces too.
- `HAIR` is the feature **hair** of hair and beards.
- `CLOTH` and `CLOTH_DARK` are the feature **clothing**.
- Leather, metal, mail, trim, boots and horn are fixed.

Keep a piece under about a dozen colours. A new colour that should be recoloured needs a feature
in `kit.py`.

## Building

```sh
python3 scripts/art/kit/build.py --out DIR            # everything: DIR/models/*.glb, DIR/kit.json
python3 scripts/art/kit/build.py --out DIR hero kit_hair_bun_10   # only these
python3 scripts/art/kit/build.py --check              # do the outputs still match manifest.json?
python3 scripts/art/kit/apply.py PORT DIR             # into the project open in an editor
```

Needs `python3` with numpy. The same sources always give the same bytes.

`apply.py` talks to an editor whose AI agent server listens on `PORT` (`mcp on PORT`). It writes
the files and the model definitions, gives armour, clothing, cloaks, helmets and hoods the style
they show when worn, and points the small races and the tiefling at their bodies. Run it twice:
once for the example (the editor started in the repository root) and once for the starter library
(the editor opened on `crates/editor/assets/starter_library` with `RUSTPG2D_FOLDER_PROJECTS=1`).

Building everything also writes `manifest.json`: the hash of the sources and of every output. A
test of the editor (`the_figure_kit_is_built_and_applied`) compares it with the files in the
starter library and the example, so a change to the kit that was not rebuilt and applied fails CI.
After applying, run `cargo run -p rustpg2d-editor --example starter_history`.

An agent connected to the editor can do the same without a shell: `kit_build` builds and imports,
`model_sheet` shows a figure as the game draws it, `model_check` lists what is wrong with a model.

## Adding to the kit

- **A hair style or beard:** a branch in `Figure.hair` / `Figure.beard`, its name in
  `HAIR_STYLES` / `BEARDS`. It is built for both head sizes.
- **An outfit:** a function in `figure.py` that returns its colours by zone (see `clothes`), its
  name in `OUTFITS` and in `OUTFIT_NAMES` of `kit.py`. It is built for every build.
- **A race:** a line in `BODIES` (its build, what it adds to the head, its skin colours). What it
  adds is drawn in `Figure.body`.
- **A build:** an entry in `BUILDS`. Every outfit and cloak is then built for it too.

Then look before you keep it: build, apply or `kit_build`, and `model_sheet` with a walk and an
attack. Check these at game size, not enlarged:

1. Does the outline say what it is?
2. Do pieces stay together when the figure walks and swings (no gaps at the joints, nothing
   poking through)?
3. Is every colour area at least two pixels wide?
4. Does `model_check` report no problems (a piece must stay under 32,768 vertices, the engine's
   limit for one draw)?
